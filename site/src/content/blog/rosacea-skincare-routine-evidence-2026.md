**Rosacea routine 2026**: a working plan is not longer or more expensive, it is more disciplined. The dos are a gentle non foaming cleanser, a ceramide and niacinamide moisturiser, and a mineral sunscreen every morning, plus one guideline-backed active matched to your subtype. The donts are fragrance, alcohol denat, menthol, eucalyptus, witch hazel, physical scrubs and any high strength AHA or BHA. This guide sorts every choice by the strength of the evidence and by rosacea subtype, using the phenotype approach now endorsed by the [National Rosacea Society](https://www.rosacea.org/) and the [European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology](https://www.eadv.org/).

> **TL;DR**: The 2026 dos are a gentle **non foaming cleanser**, a **ceramide plus niacinamide** moisturiser, a **mineral SPF 30 to 50**, and one prescription or OTC active per phenotype: azelaic acid 15 percent for papulopustular, ivermectin 1 percent for inflammatory papules, brimonidine or oxymetazoline for persistent redness, low dose isotretinoin or topical minocycline foam when guidelines allow. The donts are fragrance, alcohol denat, menthol, camphor, eucalyptus, witch hazel, sodium lauryl sulphate, physical scrubs, high strength glycolic, mandelic or salicylic acid, and hot water. Triggers such as UV, heat, alcohol and spicy food matter as much as the product list.

Rosacea affects an estimated five percent of adults worldwide and disproportionately fair skinned people between thirty and sixty, but darker phototypes are underdiagnosed rather than spared. The 2026 clinical picture is clearer than a decade ago: the [American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org/) and the National Rosacea Society now recommend a phenotype based approach, meaning the routine is chosen from the features present, such as flushing, persistent erythema, papulopustular lesions or phymatous change, rather than from a rigid subtype label. Every product referenced in this guide is graded on [SkinScore](/rankings) by INCI, concentration and tolerance profile.

## What rosacea is in 2026, and why routines still fail

Rosacea is a chronic inflammatory disease of the facial vasculature and pilosebaceous units, driven by a mix of neurovascular hyper reactivity, cathelicidin dysregulation, and, in many cases, an overgrowth of the mite Demodex folliculorum. Data reviewed by [Inserm](https://www.inserm.fr/) and summarised in the 2019 phenotype update from the [ROSacea COnsensus panel](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32064619/) confirm that the disease is not driven by a single trigger and that no cleanser or serum will silence it on its own.

Routines fail for three reasons. The first is over cleansing with foaming or scrubbing products that further destabilise a barrier that is already leaky. The second is stacking actives, especially retinoids and acids, at concentrations validated for acne but not for reactive skin. The third is treating redness cosmetically with green primers while leaving the underlying vascular reactivity untouched. The 2026 evidence based plan reverses all three moves.

## The three non negotiable dos, in order

Before any prescription or hero active, three baseline steps carry the strongest evidence and the widest consensus.

**Do use a gentle non foaming cleanser once or twice a day.** Cream or micellar formats with a pH near skin, no sulphates and no fragrance are the standard. Foaming face washes with sodium lauryl sulphate or high concentrations of sodium laureth sulphate consistently trigger stinging and worsen transepidermal water loss in rosacea prone skin, confirmed by pharmacology reviews on [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/). If you struggle to pick a product, our shortlist for [reactive skin cleansers](/blog/best-cleanser-sensitive-skin-gentle) applies directly.

**Do apply a barrier moisturiser with ceramides and niacinamide morning and night.** The combination reinforces the stratum corneum, reduces transepidermal water loss and improves tolerance to any active layered on top. Niacinamide at 2 to 5 percent has anti inflammatory and anti erythema data reviewed in dermatological research. The barrier science is covered in our guide to [ceramides and the skin barrier](/blog/ceramides-explained-why-your-barrier-needs-them).

**Do apply a mineral sunscreen every single morning.** UV is the single most consistent flare trigger. Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are inert, non irritant and unlikely to sting, unlike some chemical filters that can worsen erythema. SPF 30 to 50 with broad spectrum coverage and PPD or UVA PF at least a third of the SPF is the current [Haute Autorité de Santé](https://www.has-sante.fr/) aligned target. Formats without fragrance, without alcohol denat and with a silicone or squalane base tolerate best.

## Evidence based actives, ranked by data quality

Beyond the baseline, actives are chosen from a short list. The 2026 EADV update and the National Rosacea Society treatment algorithm converge on the same tier.

**Azelaic acid 15 percent** is the OTC and prescription workhorse for papulopustular rosacea. Randomised controlled trials show significant reduction in inflammatory lesions and erythema after eight to twelve weeks. It is anti inflammatory, mildly comedolytic and safe in pregnancy according to the [ANSM](https://ansm.sante.fr/) framework. The 10 versus 20 percent question is answered in our dedicated piece on [azelaic acid dosing](/blog/azelaic-acid-10-vs-20-who-benefits-2026), and the broader case is made in our review of [why azelaic acid is underrated for rosacea](/blog/azelaic-acid-underrated-acne-rosacea).

**Ivermectin 1 percent cream (Soolantra)** targets Demodex driven inflammatory papules and pustules. Head to head trials versus metronidazole 0.75 percent show superior lesion clearance at week 16. Prescription only in most countries, well tolerated, and the standard first line for moderate to severe papulopustular rosacea.

**Metronidazole 0.75 to 1 percent** remains a solid, well tolerated topical antibiotic with decades of data. It underperforms ivermectin on lesion count but is a reasonable, cheaper alternative and safe in pregnancy.

**Topical minocycline 1.5 percent foam** received expanded evidence in the last five years and is now a valid alternative for papulopustular rosacea in adults who cannot tolerate metronidazole or azelaic acid.

**Brimonidine 0.33 percent gel and oxymetazoline 1 percent cream** target persistent centrofacial erythema by constricting facial vessels. They do not treat inflammation and must be used with realistic expectations, including the risk of rebound flushing with brimonidine. Guidelines list them as second line, patient by patient.

**Low dose oral doxycycline 40 mg modified release** carries evidence for moderate to severe papulopustular rosacea and remains the go to systemic option when topicals plateau, per EADV and [HAS](https://www.has-sante.fr/) practice guidance.

**Isotretinoin low dose (0.1 to 0.3 mg per kg per day)** is reserved for severe or refractory phymatous or papulopustular rosacea and is a specialist decision, not a routine step.

**Niacinamide 2 to 5 percent** is not curative but supports barrier and erythema control and is the safest daily add on. The general dermatology case is summarised in our review of [niacinamide safety](/blog/is-niacinamide-safe-dermatology-research).

## The absolute donts, and why each one flares rosacea

The list of ingredients to remove is short, evidence based and non negotiable in the acute phase.

Fragrance, whether labelled parfum or as essential oils such as lavender, peppermint or eucalyptus, is the most common avoidable trigger. It sensitises, stings and can provoke allergic contact dermatitis on already inflamed skin. Our deep dive into [fragrance as a hidden allergen](/blog/fragrance-skincare-hidden-allergen) covers the mechanism. Alcohol denat and SD alcohol in leave on products dehydrate and destabilise the barrier. Menthol, camphor, peppermint oil and eucalyptus create a cold sensation by activating TRPM8 receptors, exactly the pathway involved in flushing. Witch hazel is astringent and often fragranced. Sodium lauryl sulphate strips the barrier. Physical scrubs, cleansing brushes and washcloths are mechanical trauma on skin that already vasodilates too easily. High strength glycolic, mandelic or salicylic acid, and any leave on retinoid stronger than the lowest strength adapalene or a low dose retinal, will trigger stinging in most rosacea patients and are best replaced by azelaic acid or ivermectin during flares.

## The 2026 routine, morning and night, by phenotype

**Erythematotelangiectatic phenotype (flushing and persistent redness):** morning is gentle cleanser, niacinamide serum optional, ceramide moisturiser, mineral SPF 30 to 50, brimonidine or oxymetazoline only on high stakes days. Night is gentle cleanser, ceramide moisturiser, azelaic acid 15 percent every second night if tolerated. Cold compresses on flare days.

**Papulopustular phenotype (bumps and pustules):** morning is gentle cleanser, azelaic acid 15 percent, ceramide moisturiser, mineral SPF 50. Night is gentle cleanser, ivermectin 1 percent if prescribed, ceramide moisturiser. Consider oral doxycycline 40 mg modified release during acute flares under prescription.

**Phymatous phenotype (skin thickening, mostly the nose):** requires specialist review and often combines low dose oral isotretinoin with mechanical or laser resurfacing. The at home routine remains the same three baseline steps.

**Ocular phenotype (dry, itchy, gritty eyes):** requires an ophthalmology referral, warm compresses, lid hygiene and often oral doxycycline. Topical rosacea creams do not treat the eye component and must not be applied on the lid margin unless a specialist agrees.

The order of layering within any phenotype follows the same rule as any other routine, covered in our guide to [layering order morning and night](/blog/skincare-routine-order-morning-night).

## Triggers matter as much as the shelf

The most detailed routine will underperform if daily triggers keep firing. Guidance from the National Rosacea Society and reviewed on [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) identifies UV, heat (hot baths, saunas, hot drinks), alcohol (especially red wine), spicy food, exercise without cooling, wind, and emotional stress as the top provokers. Keeping a two week trigger diary, noting the two most consistent triggers and removing them, often outperforms adding a new serum. Menopausal hot flashes and vasoactive medications should be discussed with a physician because they contribute directly to vascular reactivity.

## What to expect and when to escalate

Realistic timelines are part of the treatment. Barrier repair is felt in one to two weeks, azelaic acid reduces papules over eight to twelve weeks, ivermectin reaches its full effect around week 16, and vascular redness treated with laser or IPL needs three to five sessions. Escalate to a dermatologist if papules and pustules do not improve after twelve weeks of a full topical routine, if erythema is disabling, if the nose starts to thicken, or if the eyes are involved. The 2026 dermatology consensus, published in the EADV update, is that combining topical actives with low dose systemic therapy is safe and often necessary in moderate cases and should not wait for years of failed OTC attempts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is azelaic acid or ivermectin better for rosacea in 2026?
For papulopustular rosacea, head to head data still favours ivermectin 1 percent on lesion count and time to clearance, while azelaic acid 15 percent has broader coverage of erythema and post inflammatory pigmentation. In practice the two are often used together, ivermectin at night and azelaic acid in the morning, per EADV guidance.

### Can I still use retinol if I have rosacea?
During flares, no. In stable periods and under dermatology supervision, low doses of adapalene 0.1 percent or encapsulated retinal 0.05 to 0.1 percent can be tolerated two to three times a week over a ceramide moisturiser. Prescription tretinoin at classic 0.025 to 0.05 percent is usually too irritating for rosacea prone skin.

### Are vitamin C serums safe with rosacea?
L ascorbic acid at 10 to 20 percent frequently stings on rosacea skin because of its low pH. Better tolerated forms include sodium ascorbyl phosphate, magnesium ascorbyl phosphate and ethyl ascorbic acid at 3 to 5 percent, and they can be added once the routine is stable for at least a month.

### Do mineral sunscreens really matter more than chemical ones for rosacea?
Yes, in most cases. Zinc oxide and titanium dioxide are non irritant, non allergenic and do not generate heat on the skin. Some chemical filters, particularly older cinnamates, can sting and worsen erythema. Newer photostable filters such as bemotrizinol or bis ethylhexyloxyphenol methoxyphenyl triazine tolerate better and are a reasonable second choice if a mineral filter is cosmetically unacceptable.

### Can rosacea be cured?
No. Rosacea is a chronic condition managed in cycles of control and maintenance. The realistic goal is long remission on a minimal routine, with a hero active during flares and prevention through trigger management and daily SPF. Guidelines from the AAD, EADV and NRS are aligned on this framing.

### How is a rosacea routine different from a routine for sensitive skin without rosacea?
A generic sensitive skin routine tolerates the occasional fragrance free acid or gentle retinoid. A rosacea routine removes all foaming cleansers with SLS, all leave on alcohol denat, all menthol and camphor, and all high strength acids by default, and it always includes daily mineral SPF and a hero active matched to phenotype. The margin for error is smaller.
